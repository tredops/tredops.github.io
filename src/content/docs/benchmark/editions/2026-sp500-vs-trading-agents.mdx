Beating the market is hard. That is why index ETFs are the default answer for most investors: buy the S&P 500, keep it, and let time do the work. It is the safest bet there is — and, over any given six weeks, a bet that can simply lose.

New technology changes what "safe" can mean. A trading agent does not have to be a bigger gambler than an index; it can be a **more disciplined** one: every order it opens carries a stop loss and a take profit from the first second, it never puts more than a small slice of the capital in one position, and it can decide to do nothing when nothing convinces it. This edition puts that idea to the test.

**We gave the S&P 500 and five LLM trading agents the same $10,000 and the same 40 days. The index lost 1.04%. All five agents finished positive, between +1.96% and +13.59%, with a worst drawdown of −2.41% — smaller than the index's own.**

## The setup

- **The benchmark to beat:** a *Buy & Keep* bar that puts the full $10,000 into `SPY` on day one and never touches it. It is priced every day at the last hourly close, exactly like an open position of any agent.
- **The challengers:** the five zero-price LLM agents of the [Free agents edition](../2026-free-agents-10k-40-days/): `openai/gpt-oss-20b`, `nvidia/nemotron-3-super-120b-a12b`, `meta/muse-glimmer-30b`, `openrouter/free` and `dots-studio/dots-3-note-preview:free`. Same system prompt, same signal pool, one decision every two days.
- **Capital and risk:** $10,000 each, paper trading, max 10% of the balance per order and 20% per bot, stop loss and take profit on every order.
- **Window:** Aug 12 → Sep 21, 2026. The race below starts on Aug 17, the first day anything moved; every line is measured against the Aug 12 starting equity.

The index is the only competitor that cannot lose to itself: no fees, no slippage, no decisions. Everything it needs to win is a market that goes up.

## Results

| # | Competitor | Model | Final equity | P&L | Max drawdown | Closed trades (W / L) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | gpt-oss-20b | `openai/gpt-oss-20b` (NVIDIA) | $11,358.77 | **+13.59%** | −0.60% | 7 (5 / 2) |
| 2 | Nemotron 3 Super | `nvidia/nemotron-3-super-120b-a12b` | $10,657.91 | +6.58% | −2.41% | 12 (7 / 5) |
| 3 | Muse Glimmer | `meta/muse-glimmer-30b` (NVIDIA) | $10,590.37 | +5.90% | −1.88% | 5 (2 / 3) |
| 4 | OpenRouter free | `openrouter/free` (a router: the model changes per call) | $10,343.53 | +3.44% | −1.17% | 10 (4 / 6) |
| 5 | Dots 3 | `dots-studio/dots-3-note-preview:free` (OpenRouter) | $10,196.49 | +1.96% | −1.86% | 7 (3 / 4) |
| 6 | **S&P 500** | Buy & Keep on `SPY` | $9,896.24 | **−1.04%** | −2.40% | — |

The result is lopsided, and the way it happened matters more than the final number:

1. **The index led for two days.** SPY was up +0.65% on Aug 17 while every agent still sat at 0%. From Aug 19 on gpt-oss-20b took the lead and never gave it back; the S&P 500 was ahead of *any* agent on only 2 of the 36 days drawn.
2. **The market drifted down, and the agents did not follow it.** SPY spent the whole window between −0.2% and −1.8%. The agents were not short the market; they were mostly in cash and in a handful of positions the broker's stop loss protected. Two of them (OpenRouter free and Dots 3) stayed at exactly 0% for three weeks before their first trades.
3. **Controlled risk showed up in the drawdowns.** The deepest peak-to-trough of any agent was −2.41% (Nemotron, the most active one); the winner's was −0.60%. The index, doing nothing, drew down −2.40%. Doing nothing is not the same as risking nothing.
4. **The winner traded little.** gpt-oss-20b closed 7 trades in 40 days and finished with 6 open positions; Nemotron closed 12 and holds 20. Selectivity paid more than activity — the same lesson as the previous edition.

## Why this does not mean "agents always beat the index"

Forty days is a short window and this one was a mildly negative one for US large caps. In a straight bull run, a fully invested index compounds every day while a selective agent sits in cash waiting for a signal — and the ranking could flip. What the experiment does show is narrower and, we think, more useful: **under the same capital and the same clock, agents with mandatory stop loss / take profit and position caps produced positive returns with a smaller drawdown than passively holding the index.** That is the risk profile TredOps is built around: not a bigger bet than the market, a better-managed one.

We will keep running this comparison as a recurring series. The next edition adds paid frontier models to the same race and a longer window, so the bull-market case gets its turn.
