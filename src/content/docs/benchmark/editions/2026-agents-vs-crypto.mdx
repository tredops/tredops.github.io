What happens when you hand **nine LLM agents** $10,000 each, point them at the crypto signal pool for **36 days**, and race them against the dumbest strategy there is — buy six coins and keep them? We did exactly that between **September 1 and October 6, 2026**. The agents were not on a timer: each one woke up **only when five new signals entered the pool**, decided, and went back to sleep.

**Short answer: the basket won. Buy & Keep finished at +19.38% because the window caught a crypto rebound (AVAX +53%, AAVE +39%). The best agent — `gpt-4.1-mini` on a "moderate to high risk" prompt — made +12.45% with a maximum drawdown of 5.6% against the basket's 10.2%, never more than a quarter of the capital in the market, every position closed by the end, and a total LLM bill of $1.63.**

## The setup

- **Capital:** $10,000 per agent, paper trading, max 10% of the balance per order and 20% per bot.
- **Window:** Sep 1 → Oct 6, 2026, replayed on historical data (the agent only sees what existed on each `tradeDate`). The race chart samples every hour from Aug 31 22:00 to Oct 7 22:00 UTC — 889 frames.
- **Wake-ups:** no fixed cadence. An agent runs a cycle when **≥ 5 new signals** have entered its pool since its last wake-up (the pool is scanned hourly). 313 cycles in total, 36–40 per agent.
- **Signals:** cryptocurrency, long and short, from every production *and* staging bot of the platform, plus the orders their performance tests produced over the same dates (`marks: bot, performanceTest`). A handful of equity signals slipped through the crypto filter (ANVS, TAGS, EWY, AMOM, SIRI, AI); they are in the data and we count them.
- **Cycle prompt:** "Evaluate portfolio … Review open positions, market conditions, and the sub-agent's strategy, then decide whether to open the signal … Objective: Increase capital by 10%." Up to 7 turns per cycle.
- **Two prompts, nine agents.** Eight agents share one system prompt — a *Senior Crypto Portfolio Manager* with **capital preservation as the absolute priority**: 5–8% per position, 15–20% per sector, open only at confidence ≥ 75, "sitting in cash is a valid and often superior decision". The ninth agent runs the same prompt rewritten to be **opportunistic once the portfolio is up more than 3%**: 8–12% per position, 20–25% per sector, open at confidence ≥ 70, "do not sit excessively in cash when the portfolio is already profitable". Both prompts are published in full below.
- **Models:** `gpt-4.1-mini` (twice: one prompt each), `deepseek-v4-flash`, `deepseek-reasoner`, and five free tiers — `openai/gpt-oss-20b` and `meta/muse-glimmer-30b` on NVIDIA, `dots-studio/dots-3-note-preview:free`, `apodex/apodex-1.1-mini:free` and `liquid/lfm-2.5-2.6b:free` on OpenRouter. Temperatures 0.35–0.45, listed per agent.
- **The basket:** "Buy & Keep" is a synthetic competitor priced from the broker's hourly closes: $10,000 split across BTC, SOL, AVAX, ETH, DOGE and AAVE on the first frame and never touched again.

Its four open positions were carried passively through the rebound — an accidental Buy & Keep, not a decision. We leave it in the table because it is a result: a free tier can stop mid-race.

Four things stand out:

1. **The basket won because the window was a rebound.** Over these 36 days AVAX went from about $7.9 to $11.5 (+53%), AAVE +39%, SOL +12%, DOGE +6%, BTC +5%, ETH +4%. Hold six of them and you get +19%. Nothing an agent did could beat being fully invested in the right month — but the same basket was **−10.2%** from its peak at the worst moment, and had spent most of the year falling before September. The agents never had more than 20–25% of their capital at risk at once.
2. **The prompt mattered more than the model.** The two `gpt-4.1-mini` agents saw the same signals on the same dates. The "moderate to high risk" one (bigger positions once the portfolio is up 3%, lower confidence bar) made **+12.45%** with 64 trades; the "moderate" one made **+5.96%** with 44. The aggressive version also had four times the drawdown (−5.6% vs −1.2%) — the extra return was bought with risk, as it should be.
3. **One trade decided the podium.** Every paid agent took AVAX long on **Sep 18** at about $7.94. The aggressive gpt-4.1-mini sized it three ways (AVAX/USDC, AVAX/USDT, AVAX/USD) and collected **+$452, +$376 and +$316**, closed by take profit on Sep 21. The conservative twin took one leg for +$174 and a stop loss an hour later. DeepSeek's two agents took one leg each (+$169, +$188). Same signal, different sizing, different ranking.
4. **Free tiers were slow or stuck.** The five free models averaged 125–263 s per run (vs 30–60 s for the paid ones), one stopped at cycle 4, and together they closed 29 trades for −$259. `gpt-oss-20b` — the winner of the free-agents edition — finished last here with 3 wins in 11 trades, its worst trade a −$155 stop on an equity signal.

## Decisions worth reading

The agents write a justification for every signal they analyse. Verbatim:

> **deepseek-reasoner · Sep 21 · LTC/USD long → OPEN** — "best structure of the event. 1H trend bullish (EMA12 58.63 > EMA26 58.20), MACDh 1H +0.064 with thesis [intact] (MACD 15/30/9 hist +0.076)…" — closed by take profit the same day for **+6.9%**.

> **CryptoExpert Deepseek (deepseek-v4-flash) · Oct 1 · BTC/USD long → OPEN** — "1H trend bullish (EMA12 84499 > EMA26 84241, price above SMA50 83896), MACDh 1H +50.38 and RSI14 1H 63.0 / RSI14 1D 66.2 with headroom…"

> **deepseek-reasoner · Sep 11 · DOT/USD → SKIP** — "Es la de mejor score (14.61) y fit consistent (Calib +6.28%, Test +37.17%, 4/0), pero la entrada es tardía: el bot entra a RSI 30 y sale a 60…"

The last wake-up of the window, **Oct 6, 16:00 UTC**, is the same five signals for everyone (XTZ, LINK/USDT, LINK/USDC, SOL, DOGE — all long, all "failed breakout"). All three paid models skipped all five. The aggressive gpt-4.1-mini wrote: *"Despite the portfolio being profitable at +12.66% with solid liquidity, all these new signals exhibited bearish 1-hour trends … the portfolio's blockchain exposure is already near the maximum allowed (25%) … no new positions were opened to preserve capital."* An opportunistic prompt is still a prompt with limits.

## What we learned

- **Buy & Keep is the benchmark to beat, and in a rebound month nobody beats it.** Publishing that is the point: an agent is not magic, it is a risk policy that executes.
- **Risk policy is the product.** Same model, two prompts, 6.5 points of return apart and 4× the drawdown apart. The knobs that mattered were position size and the confidence threshold, not the model.
- **Sizing the same signal differently is the whole game.** One AVAX signal on Sep 18 explains most of the gap between first and third place among the agents.
- **Event-driven wake-ups work.** "Wake when 5 signals arrive" gave 36–40 cycles per agent in 36 days at a cost of $0.03–0.04 per cycle for the paid models, with no idle runs on empty pools.
- **Free tiers are not race-grade.** Slow, one stalled, and negative as a group. Use them to iterate on prompts, not to manage money.
- **Thirty-six days is short.** 1–64 closed trades per agent. Evidence, not a verdict.
