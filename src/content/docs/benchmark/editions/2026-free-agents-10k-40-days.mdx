Can an agent that costs **nothing** to run make money? We gave **five zero-price LLM models** the same $10,000, the same 40-day window, the same signal pool and the same system prompt, and let each one decide every two days. Only the model changed.

**Short answer: yes, all five ended positive — but the spread between the best (+13.53%) and the worst (+2.30%) is 11 points, and the winner spent the first 16 days doing nothing.**

## The setup

- **Capital:** $10,000 per agent, paper trading, max 10% of the balance per order and 20% per bot.
- **Window:** Aug 12 → Sep 21, 2026, one decision cycle every 2 days, weekends skipped → 20 cycles per agent, replayed on historical data (the agent only sees what existed on each `tradeDate`).
- **Signals:** every public production bot of the platform, crypto + ETFs + stocks, long and short.
- **Same brain, different model:** every agent runs the same system prompt (published in full below), reasoning level *medium*, up to 10 turns per cycle. Temperatures differ marginally between prompts (0.25 vs 0.30) — we list them per agent.
- **Cost:** all five models are free tiers (NVIDIA and OpenRouter). What they *would* have cost at list price is shown anyway.

Two more free models entered and were dropped, and we count that as a result: `minimaxai/minimax-m3` returned **410 Gone** (end of life on Sep 9) and its OpenRouter twin `minimax/minimax-m3:free` **404 — no longer free**; `moonshotai/kimi-k3` timed out twice at 240 s and was removed at cycle 4.

## Results

| # | Agent | Model | Final equity | P&L | Max drawdown | Trades (W / L) | Win rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | gpt-oss-20b | `openai/gpt-oss-20b` (NVIDIA) | $11,352.84 | **+13.53%** | −0.32% | 7 (5 / 2) | 71% |
| 2 | Nemotron 3 Super | `nvidia/nemotron-3-super-120b-a12b` | $10,668.45 | +6.68% | −0.69% | 12 (7 / 5) | 58% |
| 3 | Muse Glimmer | `meta/muse-glimmer-30b` (NVIDIA) | $10,572.79 | +5.73% | −1.61% | 5 (2 / 3) | 40% |
| 4 | OpenRouter free | `openrouter/free` (a router: the model changes per call) | $10,379.45 | +3.79% | −0.55% | 10 (4 / 6) | 40% |
| 5 | Dots 3 | `dots-studio/dots-3-note-preview:free` (OpenRouter) | $10,230.33 | +2.30% | −1.04% | 7 (3 / 4) | 43% |

Three things stand out:

1. **Patience beat activity.** gpt-oss-20b skipped 9 of its first 9 opportunities ("idle" cycles: nothing convincing in the pool), opened its first position on Aug 18 — AAVE/USDC long, closed by take profit for **+$653** — and then sat on that gain for ten days. Nemotron traded the most (12 closed trades) and finished second with half the return.
2. **Drawdowns were tiny.** The worst peak-to-trough of the whole race is −1.61% (Muse Glimmer). The broker's stop loss / take profit on every order did the risk management; the models mostly decided *whether* to take a signal.
3. **The router lost to the models it routes to.** `openrouter/free` picks a free model per call, so its portfolio was managed by a different "brain" every two days. It closed the most losing trades (6) and finished fourth — consistency has value.

## What each model cost

![LLM cost vs final profit](/research/2026-free-agents-10k-40-days/cost.png)

Every agent ran on a free tier, so the real bill was **$0.00**. Priced at list rates the whole race would have cost about **$2.25** across 62 LLM runs: 4.3 M input tokens for Nemotron (the chattiest, 219 tool calls) down to 0.9 M for gpt-oss-20b (82 tool calls). The winner was also the cheapest thinker.

## Decisions worth reading

The agents write a justification for every signal they analyse. Verbatim, from the transcripts:

> **OpenRouter free · Aug 18 · AAVE/USDC long → SKIP** — "trend is bullish (EMA12 88.24 > EMA26 87.56) and MACDh positive (+0.1918), so direction aligns. However, bot win rate is 0/2 (0%), backtest return +0.29% severely underperforms Buy & Hold +15.57%, risk level is high (4/5), RSI14 at 69.5 is near overbought limiting upside room, OBV negative (−0.65) shows weak volume confirmation, and no news catalyst found. Risk/reward does not justify entry."

That same AAVE/USDC signal, taken by gpt-oss-20b on the same day, became the single best trade of the race (+$653). Both readings were defensible; one of them got paid.

> **Dots 3 · Aug 30 · SOXX short → OPEN** — "bearish trend confirmed, RSI 39.6 (not oversold, room to fall), price declining from 532 to 508 over recent bars, bot win rate 67% (2/3), backtest +4.87% vs B&H +4.22%, highest score 371.87, positive alpha +4.97, low beta −0.02. Strong short signal with momentum."

> **Dots 3 · Sep 19 · UDOW short → SKIP** — "Short signal contradicts strongly bullish trend. RSI 100.0 extreme overbought but trend still up (EMA12>EMA26). Bot 0/1 win rate, profit already −3.15%. Risk 5. While overbought suggests pullback, trend is still up. Skip to avoid fighting trend."

## What we learned

- **Free is viable.** Five free models, five positive results, zero dollars. The gap to paid frontier models is a question for the next edition, not a foregone conclusion.
- **The model matters more than the prompt.** Identical instructions, identical signals: an 11-point spread comes from how each model weighs the same evidence (bot win rate, trend, risk level) — and from how long it is willing to wait.
- **Free tiers are unstable infrastructure.** Two of seven models disappeared mid-experiment (one end-of-life, one paywalled) and a third could not answer within four minutes. Anyone running free models in production needs a fallback.
- **Six weeks is short.** 20 decisions per agent, 5–12 closed trades each. Treat the ranking as evidence, not a verdict.
